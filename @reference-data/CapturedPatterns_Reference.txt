==============================================================================
DSL2 CAPTURED GAME-ECHO PATTERNS — full community reference
==============================================================================
Generated 2026-07-15 for Dark and Shattered Lands (DSL), a MUD running on
Mudlet 4.20.1. Compiled by scanning:
  (1) DSL2's own .lua files (the active, in-use trigger set),
  (2) DSL2's own native Mudlet XML trigger tree (current/*.xml — objects
      built in the Trigger Editor, separate from the .lua layer),
  (3) the vendored PNP package source ("PNP files/", 46 files — DSL's
      long-standing community client, the origin of most of DSL2's own
      patterns),
  (4) sibling Mudlet profiles on the same machine (other DSL clients/
      characters) for anything not already covered above.

Patterns are Mudlet's PCRE regex dialect unless marked "(literal/substring)"
— those match as plain text, not regex, in the native Trigger Editor.

Sections marked **[ACTIVE — DSL2]** are live in DSL2 today (source: its own
.lua files). Sections marked **[NATIVE ONLY]** exist as native Mudlet
Trigger Editor objects in this profile (or a sibling) but have NOT been
ported into DSL2's own MyDSL_*.lua capture layer — real, confirmed DSL text,
but not currently driving any MyDSL window/state. Sections marked **[PNP
SOURCE]** are from the community PNP package, included for completeness/
cross-reference even where DSL2 built its own independent implementation.

HOW TO SEARCH THIS CORPUS WITHOUT GETTING FALSE NEGATIVES. An earlier pass on
2026-08-02 marked four entries CORPUS-ABSENT and three of those calls were
WRONG — "You fall flat on your face!" has 243 occurrences, the neuter bash
variant has 641, and the gore line has 1,482. Two of this doc's own long-
standing "zero corpus occurrences" notes were wrong the same way: the Unholy
proc has 96,825 and the Mana-drain proc 128,796. Three traps caused it:

  1. THE QUOTES ARE THIS DOCUMENT'S, NOT THE GAME'S. Entries below are written
     as "You fall flat on your face!" — searching for the quoted form finds
     nothing. Strip the quotes first.
  2. ANSI COLOUR SITS INSIDE PHRASES. DSL colours mid-sentence, so a literal
     search for a whole phrase can fail on a line that plainly contains it.
     Strip \x1b[...m before matching.
  3. HALF THE CORPUS IS JSON. The *.jsonl and raw.log files hold game text as
     escaped JSON strings (, \r, \"), and some use a ¤M= framing. Unescape
     before matching, or those files silently contribute nothing.

Also beware over-broad probes in the other direction: a search for "your
master" is dominated by the MASTER ACCOUNT login prompt ("What is your Master
Account's name?"), not by pet dialogue.

Two corpora were used, and the difference between them matters:
  - Text corpus: C:\Projects\DSL\GameLogs\ShatteredArchive\Docker\game-server
    (~500k text lines, 2026-07-01..19) — answers "does this wording exist".
  - Protocol corpus: the same tree's *.jsonl records, which interleave raw
    text, GMCP packages and client input in true wire order with millisecond
    timestamps — answers "what arrives WHEN, relative to what". The plain
    GameLog-DSL_*.txt files cannot answer the second kind of question at all,
    since they carry no GMCP; see section 7.

A companion file, PatternMap.yaml, maps these patterns to what a text-to-
speech reader should SAY for each (utterance-first, so many patterns can
share one utterance), and records subject/actor capture positions that a
flat pattern list has nowhere to put.

1. WEAPON-FLAG PROCS
==============================================================================
One-letter flag codes attribute a proc to the most recent attacker/target/
weapon-noun. **[ACTIVE — DSL2]** (MyDSL_DataLayer.lua ~3349-3439), ported
from and confirmed identical to **[PNP SOURCE]** (DSL_PNP_Battle.lua's
make_triggers(), Flag Trigger table) except where noted.

C — Frost:
  ^([\w\-\s,'"]+) freezes ([\w\-\s,'"]+)\.$
  ^The cold touch of ([\w\-\s,']+) surrounds you with ice

F — Flaming:
  ^([\w\-\s,']+) is burned by ([\w\-\s,']+)\.$
  ^([\w\-\s,']+) sears your flesh

L — Shocking:
  ^([\w\-\s,']+) is struck by lightning from ([\w\-\s,']+)\.$
  ^([\w\-\s,']+) is shocked by a

H — Vampiric:
  ^([\w\-\s,'"]+) draws life from ([\w\-\s,'"]+)\.$
  ^You feel ([\w\-\s,']+) drawing your life away

S — Stunning:
  ^([\w\-\s,'"]+) is knocked to the ground by ([\w\-\s,'"]+)\.$

M — Mana drain:  **[CORPUS-VERIFIED 2026-08-02 — the "zero occurrences" note
                  below was a false negative; this is one of the most common
                  procs in the whole corpus]**
  ^You feel something drawing your energy away          (self; 351 hits)
  ^([\w\-\s,']+) draws energy from ([\w\-\s,']+)\.$     (128,796 hits)
      Group 1 is the WEAPON, group 2 the victim — e.g. "A dreadwood
      quarterstaff draws energy from a disinterested yinn warrior."

O — Holy:
  ^You feel a surge of ([\w\-\s,']+)'s holy wrath race through your body
  ^A flash of holy power erupts from ([\w\-\s,']+) and hits ([\w\-\s,']+)!$

U — Unholy:  **[CORPUS-VERIFIED 2026-08-02 — both lines; the "zero
              occurrences" note was a false negative, and the third-person
              line was MISSING from this doc entirely]**
  ^You feel a surge of ([\w\-\s,']+)'s unholy wrath race through your body   (1,107 hits)
  ^A flash of unholy power erupts from ([\w\-\s,']+) and hits ([\w\-\s,']+)!$ (96,825 hits)
      Exactly mirrors the Holy pair above, as symmetry suggested. Group 1 is
      the WEAPON, group 2 the victim — e.g. "A flash of unholy power erupts
      from a dreadwood quarterstaff and hits a merman!"

P — Poison (DSL2's own addition — no PNP equivalent, confirmed via sibling-profile logs):
  ^([\w\-\s,']+) coats ([\w\-\s,']+) with deadly lifebane poison\.$
      **[MEANING CLARIFIED 2026-08-02]** This is NOT a proc landing on a victim.
      Group 1 is a PLAYER and group 2 is their own WEAPON — "Karotte coats a
      grand arcanium dagger with deadly lifebane poison." is someone applying
      poison to a blade, checked against all 16 distinct corpus wordings. It
      belongs with item/prep actions, not with the two victim-side lines below.
  ^([\w\-\s,']+) is poisoned by the venom on ([\w\-\s,']+)\.$
  ^([\w\-\s,']+) shivers and suffers\.$

Confirmed non-functional / no echo at all:

-- Room render vs. room_data — ordering  **[CORPUS-VERIFIED 2026-08-02]** --

A room render is a fixed four-part structure, in this order:

  <title>                      column 0, and it is EXACTLY the string
                               gmcp.room_data reports as `room`
  <description>                2-space indent, word-wrapped over many lines
   [Exits: north east ... ]    one leading space
  <objects and creatures>      objects 5-space indented, creatures column 0
                               (both optional)

A render is ALWAYS immediately followed by gmcp.char_data then gmcp.room_data,
then the prompt. The gap from the render's first line to its room_data is
p50 1ms, p95 5ms, p99 8ms, max 89ms (n=31,185).

THE TITLE IS OFTEN NOT THE FIRST LINE AFTER THE PROMPT. Movement, look and
portal commands all behave identically here, so this is not a per-command
quirk — the render simply gets whatever else the server had to say first:

  "You enter a dark glowing orb."          portals/enter (23% of those renders)
  "You step through a gate and vanish."    gate/summon
  "You wake and stand up."                 waking before moving
  "Just as you start to leave, a fountain of blood erupts from the altar."
                                           failed-exit flavor
  a chat or OOC line that landed mid-render
  an entire multi-room sequence            river drift, speedwalk, ship travel

Measured over 298,141 prompt-delimited windows containing 37,997 renders:
15.7% of renders are preceded by other output. A consumer that anchors on the
first line after the prompt therefore misses 6,170 of them — 16.2%, one room
in six. Scanning the window for a line that IS the room name instead (last
match, since a window can hold several renders) misses 283, or 0.74%; those
remaining are scrying ("You peer into a shimmering gate.") and ship movement,
where room_data reports YOUR room while the text describes a different one.

THE CONVERSE IS FALSE, and it is the trap worth documenting. room_data does
not mark a room render — it fires before EVERY prompt. Across 207 docker
*.jsonl logs and 341,481 room_data events:

  9.1%   (31,185)   followed an actual room render
  78.9%  (269,276)  followed unrelated output — equipment lists, auction
                    chat, "You have become better at pugil!", "You are
                    thirsty.", "You lost your concentration."
  12.0%  (41,020)   followed no text at all (consecutive prompts)

So the event's arrival confirms nothing. What discriminates is the NAME:
somewhere in the window is a line that IS room_data's `room` value, and the
render starts there. Whole-line equality only — containment would let a chat
line quoting a room name open a render. Measured, that separates them
cleanly: every matched window also carried an [Exits:] line, bar 20 short
brief-mode renders that legitimately have none.

Practical consequence for any real-time consumer: room_data is a TRAILING
marker. It can confirm text that already arrived but cannot announce text
that is coming, so a consumer that must classify a description has to hold
lines briefly and decide on the name — it cannot decide on sight, because
nothing in the text itself distinguishes a room description from any other
prose.
